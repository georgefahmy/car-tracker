Detection Engine & Performance

Object detector weights. YOLO26n and YOLO11n are fastest on CPU; Small (s) and D-FINE transformer models trade higher CPU usage for better accuracy on small or occluded vehicles.
Execution runtime for inference. OpenVINO Intel iGPU (/dev/dri) runs native FP16 with a persistent GPU shader cache; Auto prefers Intel iGPU / MPS / CUDA when present, then falls back to CPU.
Longest-edge resolution cap for YOLO inference, automatically matched to your stream's aspect ratio without upscaling Sub Streams (D-FINE runs at fixed 640x640).
Maximum frames per second (0.5–30) analyzed during active motion. 3–7 FPS tracks street traffic smoothly while keeping CPU temperatures low.
Minimum detection score (0.05–0.95) required to track and count an object. Increase (0.35–0.45) to reduce false positives; lower (0.20–0.25) for dark or distant views.
Maximum pixel width of the live annotated JPEG feed in the browser. Lowering saves network bandwidth without affecting AI detection or full-res snapshots.
Visual size multiplier (0.4–3.0) for bounding boxes and text labels drawn on the live preview and saved violation snapshots.
Uses fast OpenCV pixel-diff checks (ignoring Exclusion Masks) to throttle neural-net inference to ~0.5 FPS when the street is static, waking instantly to full FPS on motion while keeping active Red-Zone timers ticking.

Red-Zone, Speeding & Emergency Rules

Seconds a vehicle must stay stationary inside a Red Zone before logging an illegal parking violation, snapshot, and alert (total parked duration keeps counting afterward).
Normalized frame fraction (0.001–0.2) a parked vehicle's anchor may jitter without resetting its dwell timer. Default 0.015 (~1.5% of frame) absorbs minor box wobble.
Speed threshold in mph between two enabled Traffic Flow Lines (requires 3D Ground Grid calibration) that triggers a speeding violation.
Captures a high-resolution annotated snapshot, logs a violation, and sends optional webhook/HA events whenever a vehicle exceeds the Speed Limit.
Runs local RapidOCR license plate recognition on demand when a Red-Zone or Speeding snapshot is saved (0% continuous stream CPU cost).
Skips Red-Zone violation alerts when roof emergency lightbars, flashing strobes, or fire-engine red bodies are detected using the thresholds below.
Minimum HSV color saturation (100–255) for vivid red/blue roof lightbars. Higher values prevent sun glare or reflections from matching.
Minimum HSV brightness (120–255) for active emergency beacons so dark red or blue paint is not mistaken for lit strobes.
Minimum frame-to-frame bright-pixel ratio change (0.002–0.15) in the upper vehicle region required to detect flashing strobe lights.
Minimum fraction of vivid red body pixels (0.10–0.80) on a truck or bus to classify it as a fire engine even without active strobes.

Scheduled Person Watch

Triggers a photo snapshot, increments Person Zone Alerts, and sends webhook/HA events whenever a person stays inside watched zones (or full camera view) during the active schedule.
Local 24-hour clock start time when Person Watch activates (e.g. 00:00 or 23:00). Cross-midnight windows such as 23:00–05:00 are supported.
Local 24-hour clock end time when Person Watch turns off (e.g. 05:00). Equal start and end times monitor 24/7.
Minimum seconds a person must remain inside the watched area before triggering a snapshot and alert (0 = immediate; walking does not reset the timer).
Area monitored during active hours: drawn Person Zones if present else full view, strictly Person Zones, existing Vehicle Red Zones, or always Full Camera View.

Optional Discord, Slack, or custom JSON webhook URL for instant Red-Zone & Speeding alerts (with image attachments on Discord) and daily summary reports.

Local 24-hour clock time (e.g. 23:55) when the automated End-of-Day Summary Report is sent and daily counters reset.
